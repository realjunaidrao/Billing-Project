5 Denial Codes Costing US Practices Thousands Every Month

CO-97, CO-96, CO-18 and friends: what the most expensive denial codes really mean, why they keep recurring, and the exact fixes that stop them.

Every US practice knows the sting: the claim was clean, the service was real, and the payer still said no. Across the hundreds of billing portfolios we've audited, a handful of denial codes show up again and again — and they quietly drain five and six figures a year from practices that never trace them to the root.

Here are the five most expensive repeat offenders, what they actually mean, and how to stop them.

1. CO-97 — Benefit for this service is included in another payment

This is the bundling denial. The payer believes your procedure was part of another service on the same claim and refuses to pay it separately. The fix: check NCCI edits before submission, use the correct modifiers (59, XE, XP, XS, XU) only when documentation truly supports them, and appeal with operative notes highlighting distinct procedural work.

2. CO-96 — Non-covered charges

The payer says the service isn't covered under the patient's plan. Most CO-96 denials we see are actually eligibility failures — nobody verified coverage before the visit. The fix: real-time eligibility and benefits verification before every appointment, plus ABNs where appropriate.

3. CO-18 — Duplicate claim or service

You billed something the payer thinks it already processed. Often caused by resubmitting without checking claim status first. The fix: check claim status before any resubmission, and use frequency codes (7/8) for corrected claims instead of sending fresh ones.

4. CO-16 — Claim lacks information / CO-197 — Authorization absent

Missing data and missing prior authorizations are the two most preventable denial categories in existence. The fix: pre-visit auth checks tied to the schedule, and claim-scrubbing rules that catch missing fields before the claim leaves your system.

5. CO-29 — Timely filing expired

The claim arrived after the payer's deadline — and timely filing denials are almost never overturned. The fix: daily claim submission, a work queue sorted by filing deadline, and weekly AR reviews that flag anything approaching the limit.

The pattern behind the pattern

Notice something? Four of these five are process failures, not payer cruelty. That's why our denial work always pairs appeals (recover today's dollars) with prevention (fix the workflow so next quarter's denials never happen). Practices that do both typically see denial rates fall by a third or more within two quarters.
