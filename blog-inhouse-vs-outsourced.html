In-House vs Outsourced Medical Billing: The Real Cost Comparison

A $60k biller vs 5% of collections vs a $1,500/mo specialist — honest math on what each model really costs a practice doing $1M in collections.

"Should we hire a biller or outsource?" It's the most common question practice owners ask us. Let's do honest math for a practice collecting $1M a year.

Option A: In-house biller — ~$72,000+/year all-in

A $55–60k salary becomes $70k+ with benefits, payroll tax, training and turnover. You get one person, one skill set, 40 hours a week — and when they're on vacation or quit, billing stops. Management time is extra.

Option B: US billing company — $70,000–90,000/year

At the typical 7–9% of collections, a $1M practice pays $70–90k — often plus setup fees and annual contracts. You get a team, but at the highest price point and frequently the least flexibility.

Option C: Zaxis Health complete RCM — $50,000/year

At 5% of net collections: $50k on $1M collected. Full team, full cycle, month-to-month after a 90-day ramp, no setup fees. And because it's a percentage of collections, we only earn more when you do.

Option D: Dedicated specialist — $18,000/year

A full-time billing specialist working only your accounts for $1,500/month. Compare that with $5,000+/month for an in-house hire. Best for practices that want control plus leverage.

The decision framework

Stay in-house if you have a genuinely excellent biller and clean metrics to prove it. Outsource fully if billing is a distraction, AR is climbing, or you're growing. Go hybrid (dedicated specialist) if you want your hands on the wheel with an expert engine underneath.
