How Medicare Prices Lab Tests (CLFS)

Most clinical lab tests aren't priced like physician services. Here's the 60-second version:

⚖️

Weighted median, not RVUs

Under PAMA, Medicare pays the weighted median of private-payer rates that labs report — not a resource-based fee schedule. Rates update on a 3-year cycle.

📄

Find your exact rate

Every code's national payment amount lives in the CMS CLFS data files, updated quarterly. Always verify the current file before quoting or appealing.

🔗

Official source

Check any code's current Medicare rate in the official CMS Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule files.

Open CMS CLFS files →
Why this matters for revenue: when payers underpay against CLFS or your contract, the difference is recoverable — but only if you reconcile payments to the schedule. Most labs don't.
Lab billing pitfalls

Where Labs Lose Money

🧬

MolDx & panel stacking

In MolDx jurisdictions, molecular panels face strict coverage rules. Stacking per-organism codes (like 87798 × N) without meeting criteria is a top audit trigger.

📦

Unbundled panels

Billing component codes alongside the panel code that includes them — payers see it, deny it, and flag the account.

📝

Missing medical necessity

Screening diagnosis on a diagnostic test (or vice versa) is the #1 lab denial driver. The diagnosis must match the test's coverage rules.

CLIA & clean billing

CLIA SOPs That Protect Your Revenue

Most lab billing shortfalls trace back to compliance gaps, not payer stinginess.

🏅

Match billing to your CLIA certificate

Certificate of Waiver, PPM, Compliance, or Accreditation — bill only tests your certificate level covers. Billing non-waived tests on a waiver certificate is an automatic denial and a compliance risk.

📋

SOP essentials

Written test-ordering protocols, documented medical necessity for every order, ABN on file before non-covered testing, and specimen chain-of-custody. Auditors ask for these first.

💰

Close the shortfall loop

Reconcile every remittance against the CLFS and your payer contracts. Underpayments and silent downcodes are where lab revenue quietly leaks — a monthly reconciliation worklist recovers it.

Fraud-prevention rule: never upcode drug-test tiers (G0480–G0483) or stack per-organism molecular codes beyond what was performed and documented. Clean coding is the cheapest compliance program there is.
